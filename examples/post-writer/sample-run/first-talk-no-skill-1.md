Yesterday I gave my first conference talk. 25 minutes on stage.

At minute 3, my slides failed.

I had a choice: stop, wait for the tech to get fixed, or keep going without them. I kept going.

I didn't have my visual cues, so I leaned harder on the story and on talking to the room instead of reading a screen. It was the most uncomfortable stretch of the talk, and it's where people said I was at my best.

A few things I took away:

- Know your material well enough that slides are a support, not a script.
- Technical failures are going to happen. How you recover matters more than avoiding them.
- Nerves feel worse from the inside than they look from the audience.

Thank you to everyone who came up afterward to say hello. And to whoever is reading this and has a talk coming up: you can do it without the slides. You probably already know more than you think.
