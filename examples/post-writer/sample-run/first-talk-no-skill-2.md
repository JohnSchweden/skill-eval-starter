Gave my first conference talk yesterday: 25 minutes, and my slides failed at minute 3.

So I talked without them.

I had prepared for the talk, but not for the moment when the screen went blank. What I had left was the thing I actually knew: the ideas, the stories behind them, and why they mattered to me. Once I stopped looking at the screen and started talking to the room, the talk got better.

Afterward, several people told me the part without slides was the best part. I'm still processing that, but the lesson is clear: the slides supported the talk. I was the talk.

If you're preparing your first talk, know your material well enough to give it with no slides at all. You may need that backup someday, and it helps even when everything works.

To everyone who came up afterward to say something kind: thank you. This was my first, and it won't be my last.
